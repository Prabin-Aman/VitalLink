Type 2 diabetes affects how the body uses insulin. Day-to-day habits strongly influence glucose levels, alongside any medication your care team prescribes.

## Habits that help

- **Regular meals** with fibre-rich carbohydrates, protein and vegetables help avoid large swings.
- **Movement after meals:** a 10–15 minute walk can lower the rise in glucose after eating.
- **Sleep:** short or broken sleep can make glucose harder to manage.
- **Medication on schedule:** VitalLink can remind you and record each dose.

## Keeping an eye on your numbers

If you use a meter or a continuous glucose monitor, note how meals, activity and stress affect your readings. Bring that pattern to appointments rather than single values.

## Know the warning signs

Low blood sugar can cause shakiness, sweating, confusion or a racing heart. Follow the plan your clinician gave you for treating a low, which usually means fast-acting carbohydrate followed by a recheck.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Don't change medication without talking to your care team.*
