Most adults need **seven or more hours** of sleep a night on a regular basis. Needs vary a little from person to person, but routinely sleeping less than seven hours is linked with poorer concentration, mood and long-term health.

## Signs you may be short on sleep

- You rely on an alarm every day and still wake up tired.
- You doze off easily in the afternoon or while reading.
- You sleep much longer on days off than on work days.

## What usually helps

1. **Keep a steady schedule.** Going to bed and waking at about the same time, weekends included, matters more than any single early night.
2. **Protect the last hour.** Dim lights and put screens away. Your Digital wellbeing tab shows how much you use your phone after 10 PM.
3. **Watch caffeine and alcohol.** Caffeine can linger for six hours or more; alcohol fragments sleep later in the night.
4. **Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet.**

## Using your sleep data

Wearables estimate sleep stages from movement and heart rate. Treat the numbers as a trend rather than an exact measurement: a week of shorter nights is more meaningful than one low score.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. If you snore loudly, stop breathing in your sleep or feel sleepy despite enough time in bed, talk to a clinician.*
